# Prior projects digest: bc22 → bc26 → bc25 (the first three attempts)

Sources: the filtered copies under `~/projects/vibe/reference/readroom-no2019/`
(`battlecode22-vibe/`, `battlecode26-vibe/`, `battlecode25-vibe/`). Paths below are
relative to those three roots: `22:`, `26:`, `25:`. Logs were grepped, not read whole.
Attempt order was 22, then 26, then 25. Later projects get more weight.

Headline numbers, used throughout:
- **bc22**: 131 logged iterations, about 62 accepted. Never beat the strongest finalist
  benchmark (`sample_camelcase`), going 0/20 for the whole project (22:LEARNINGS.md:520-527).
  Against `sample_afinals` it plateaued at about 15-20%. By the end, the current bot won only 50%
  against snapshots g_iter41 and g_iter51, which were 15 or more accepts older
  (22:progress/vs_old_bots_history.csv).
- **bc26**: 251 iterations and 34 accepted snapshots. It won about 5% (8-10 of 162 games)
  against vendored tournament bots, killed 7 enemy rats for every 347 it lost, and won only on
  3 of 27 maps (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:873-918).
- **bc25**: four lineages. alice, bob and carol developed under strict mutual isolation from
  09-06 to 09-10. At best they won 17.3%, 4.7% and 24.7% against the finalist yardstick `v3`,
  and 0-1.3% against `TSPAARKHS`. **darla**, a from-scratch rewrite that combined every
  lineage's *measured* findings, scored 34.0% against `v3` on its first build. After 193 arms
  and 7 accepts it reached **56.0%** (25:benchmarks/HISTORY.md "Records vs v3").
  This is the single most important result in the three projects.

## 1. Training method in each, and how it evolved

### bc22: the original "Gauntlet loop" (22:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md)
- **Gauntlet** = every opponent × every map × both sides, which is 2·B·N games (:49-53). The
  pool grows with every accepted iteration plus external bots found on GitHub (:55-60).
- Opponents are split into **peers** (we win 30-90%), which gate acceptance, and
  **benchmarks** (we win under 30%), which are scoreboards that never gate. Benchmarks are played
  every 3rd Gauntlet (:62-75). An opponent is retired after we beat it ≥80% in two consecutive
  runs; the owner lowered this from 90% (:77-85, 22:TRAINING_LOG.md:2900).
- **Diff runs game by game against the baseline** (the last accepted iteration's own run) and
  read the diff's *shape*. Scattered flips in mixed directions are noise. A one-directional
  shift concentrated on one map or side across many opponents is a real regression (:87-95).
- Iteration 0 is a minimal random mover with replay instrumentation and **live bytecode
  monitoring from day one** (:97-101).
- Hyperparameters: WinPct 60%, MaxHypothesis 10, MaxSolutions 10, BenchmarkEvery 3,
  ReproSampleSize 8, NearMiss 5 points with 3 extra refinements, MaxConsecutiveRejects 3
  (:105-114).
- Loop: lose a game, form a hypothesis with pre-stated variables and thresholds, re-run it, then
  run a generality check on another losing game and an "act-on-it" check against the log's
  history. Then build a solution and verify it **mechanistically** with three possible outcomes:
  won; still lost but engaged as designed with an evidenced reason it can't flip; or no evidence,
  so undo. Then an 8-peer repro sample, then the full Gauntlet (:116-165).
- Later additions, all of them owner-driven:
  - "never idle" and "MaxConsecutiveRejects forces a change of area" (:127, 22:TRAINING_LOG.md:9157)
  - **High-risk structural exploration** as a first-class track (:130-139, 22:TRAINING_LOG.md:2668)
  - a replay archive per iteration (:171-185, 22:TRAINING_LOG.md:2297)
  - round-count deltas as a supporting signal (:187-206)
  - a fixed every-10th old-bot roster (:208-233)
- The algorithm was rewritten mid-project to match practice. Accepting without winning the
  motivating game, based on mechanistic evidence, had become routine (22:TRAINING_LOG.md:8577).

### bc26: bc22's loop plus a measurement-science layer (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md)
- The bc22 core was carried over unchanged (:1-12). Additions:
  - a mirror-match check for play symmetry (:42-50)
  - **synthetic archetypes** covering the year's strategy space when no external bots exist
    (:91-117)
  - tracking win *type*, not just win/loss (:233-238)
- After 2026-09-03 (:273-322), **instruments are ranked by how even the matchup is.** An
  instrument pinned near 0% or 100% cannot resolve small changes, so the mirror or head-to-head
  becomes primary. But "resolution ≠ representativeness": the mirror proved a trap ring
  "worthless" because our lineage never rushes, while the benchmark early-wipe rate rose from
  16% to 26% without it.
- Three free checks (:324-350):
  - arm-to-arm identity: 162/162 identical games means the change never ran
  - per-round normalisation of counters
  - timestamp correlation before blaming a mechanism
- **Dose-response, always with a zero arm.** The engine is deterministic, so re-running gives
  nothing new (:352-371).
- **Ablate already-accepted features** (:373-402).
  - The two most valuable features turned out to be failure-mode preventers: about +28 and +24.
  - A feature accepted at a "95%" headline was worth about 0. The trap ring was negative.
- **Primary accept test: head-to-head against the latest accepted snapshot**, because the
  archetypes go stale or collapse to 50% (:435-464).
- Reachability pre-check (:567-610), measure shared capped pools (:612-634), and run
  `vs_old_bots` *before* accepting when margins are thin (:657-691).
- An atomic post-accept routine (:693-747) and a "closed directions" ledger with re-open
  conditions (:801-871).
- Ending state: "nothing incremental left; rewrite or harden" (:924-926).

### bc25: a year-agnostic doctrine, multiple agents, then a rewrite (25:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md, 25:MULTI_AGENT.md)
- A fresh synthesis of bc22 and bc26 (:1-14). Standing rules: no bot downloads from the web, and
  no current-year post-mortems (25:README.md:14-16).
- **Phase 0, ground truth before strategy** (:18-81):
  - spec digest
  - `javap` and engine-source probes
  - an API-unused sweep at iteration 5 and every 10 after
  - checks for radius asymmetries
  - infrastructure first (a raw-`java` runner took per-game time from about 10-30 s to about 2 s)
  - verify determinism
  - bytecode monitoring in iteration 0
  - a symmetry audit
  - knowing the map contract
- **20 measurement doctrines** (:139-358). The key ones:
  - zero arm
  - the identity check
  - regime-matched samples
  - the wrong-referent error
  - "a flagged caveat is not discharged"
  - head-to-head as primary
  - binomial noise is the wrong model
  - replay state is recorded post-action
  - "a lesson you wrote is not a control"
  - pooled three-body tournament rates
- Pre-checks before building (:414-497):
  - reachability at three levels: does the branch fire, how big is the choice set, does the
    choice set carry the signal
  - trigger frequency, generality and history
  - check evidence already on disk
  - price reallocations against what they displace
  - instrument the decision, not the outcome
- Staged evaluation (:529-579): a one-map identity check, then the repro sample, then the full
  run plus head-to-head. "Result holds, explanation fails → accept, attribution OPEN."
- **§5b (:586-666): the head-to-head gate measures a partial derivative.** Accepts do not add up
  (+8 then +6 gave +8 overall, not +14), so a chain of accepts can drift downhill. Only the frozen
  roster sees this. Ablate in pairs when the roster drops.
- **Multi-agent experiment** (25:MULTI_AGENT.md):
  - alice, bob and carol, isolated, with a 450-game round-robin twice a day as the only shared
    channel
  - bob retired on 09-10, alice and carol on 09-14 (:56-72)
  - darla started on 09-10 with everything visible (25:agents/darla/DESIGN.md:1-25)
- Darla's loop, a lighter evolution:
  - every change is an "arm" (`make-arm.sh`) in a queue (`arm-runner.sh`)
  - registration is pre-written with a falsifier
  - **paired head-to-head on all 75 maps** with McNemar on discordant (map, side) keys
  - an idle-filler that runs fresh random-map roster samples overnight
  - `accept-iteration.sh` does the freeze/promote/benchmark steps mechanically
- On 09-14 the owner allowed accepting on a significant win against `v3` OR the roster, with
  z > 2 on discordant keys (darla DESIGN.md:5907-5945, 9052-9070).

### Evolution in one line each
- **22**: win-rate gate plus mechanistic verification, then diff shape, then "never idle,
  go bold".
- **26**: noticed the gate itself was lying (stale archetypes, lopsided instruments, never-firing
  arms), so it built measurement hygiene and ablation. The bot stayed weak.
- **25**: codified the hygiene into doctrine, then over-elaborated it: METHODS.md grew to 86
  entries and 1065 lines, plus 2544 lines of evidence. The three isolated lineages co-adapted to a
  joint local optimum (25:OBJECTIVE.md:26-50). A rewrite that pooled measured facts plus a
  sensitive paired instrument (darla) is what actually moved absolute strength.

## 2. What worked (evidence), ranked

1. **A from-scratch rewrite that combined measured findings from many lineages.** It beat weeks
   of incremental work on day 1.
   - Darla v1 scored 34.0% against `v3`, where the best incremental lineage had 24.7%
     (25:benchmarks/HISTORY.md).
   - It was built on 9 measured findings, each mapped to a code decision
     (25:agents/darla/DESIGN.md:27-49).
   - Its core was carol's economy plus a siege fork carol had built and never adopted. That
     combination alone was worth about +12 points against carol (DESIGN.md:578-584).
   - All three RESEARCH syntheses already recommended this: "rewrite rather than retrofit"
     (26:RESEARCH.md:191-201, 25:reference/RESEARCH.md:367-379).
2. **Paired head-to-head of candidate against baseline on the full map pool.**
   - 27 darla arms were screened on a 3-opponent gauntlet and every one read as noise
     (difference of differences, about a 5-7 point floor).
   - The first direct head-to-head scored 97-53, z=+3.59 (DESIGN.md:1616-1660).
   - "The bottleneck was never the VM or the ideas; it was measuring the wrong difference."
   - bc26 reached the same conclusion: head-to-head is primary (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:435-464).
3. **Trace a real replay to find a stall or degenerate state.** Don't theorise.
   - bc22: "nearly every serious bug was found by reading `--metrics/--indicators/--moves`"
     (22:LEARNINGS.md:484-491).
   - bc26: all of 09-03's accepts came from per-100-round activity traces showing a stall, such
     as the pop cap blocking builds while rich, or a dead band between two thresholds
     (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:404-418).
4. **Failure-mode preventers and "capability at zero marginal cost".**
   - bc22: a sticky committed mining target took the win rate from 60.3% to 69.7% in one change
     (22:LEARNINGS.md:203-212). An Archon heal-vs-build fix found 6815 lead hoarded, unspent
     (22:TRAINING_LOG.md:7185).
   - bc26: of the six ablated features, the two worth anything (+28, +24) were degenerate-state
     detectors. "Three changes that ever paid share one property: capability preserved at ZERO
     marginal cost" (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:373-402, 891-895).
   - bc26: the King attacked instead of building the moment an enemy arrived. Building before
     attacking fixed early wipes (26:TRAINING_LOG.md:6630-6680).
   - darla: "don't queue at a dry tower" gave +12 games (DESIGN.md:3356).
5. **Find where the game is decided, then compare economy and army scale against a stronger
   opponent.**
   - darla tallied win types: 96.5% of games were decided by paint coverage, which refuted its
     own founding thesis on day 1 (DESIGN.md:230-280).
   - Against `v3`: our units were 70% *more* productive each, but `v3` fielded 2.2× the army
     because it built 4× the money towers (DESIGN.md:9794-9850).
   - `v3` had its first tower at r34; ours came at r266. "The race is decided before round 100"
     (DESIGN.md:5825-5864). A one-constant opening fix ("soldiers until the third tower") was
     accepted as i5.
   - bc22 says the same: "raw mass frequently beats economic sophistication", and the combat
     gaps were really production/Archon-count gaps (22:LEARNINGS.md:378-414).
6. **Dose-response with a zero arm, plus ablation of carried features.**
   - In bc26 a 4-cheese arm looked bad against 16 cheese, but measuring zero showed an interior
     optimum at 4 (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:360-364).
   - darla's last accept was a monotone dose curve: RUIN_BAN 250→10 moved the score
     72→74→76→77→84 (DESIGN.md:11585-11605).
7. **Engine probes and API-unused sweeps.**
   - bc22: `mutate()` went unused for 81 iterations and PORTABLE/TURRET semantics were missed
     until a `javap` sweep (22:LEARNINGS.md:41-58).
   - bc26: a capability audit after 105 iterations found `becomeRatKing`, `carry/throwRat` and
     `squeak` had never been called. Opponents were already using throws
     (26:TRAINING_LOG.md:7186-7230).
   - The sweep is now scheduled, at iteration 5 and every 10 after
     (25:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:31-45).
8. **Play-symmetry audit.**
   - bc22 measured a side bias of 71.4% as A versus 43.6% as B over 720 games, with maps like
     maptestsmall showing 3% vs 100% losses by side (22:TRAINING_LOG.md:10331-10350).
   - Fixing 8 fixed-compass tie-break sites halved the gap and *raised* the win rate
     (22:LEARNINGS.md:495-519).
9. **Bug-nav as an escape hatch bolted onto greedy movement, not as a replacement.**
   - Replacing the fallback cost −14 games. Firing the wall-hug only after a measured "stuck"
     condition gave +13 (darla DESIGN.md:3823-3880).
10. **Self-calibrating thresholds.** Counting observed raid events in-game beat four fixed-constant
    variants (22:LEARNINGS.md:213-227).
11. **Machine never idle, independent of the session.**
    - setsid-detached queue runner with flock, plus an idle-filler, plus a monitor that filters
      on failure signatures too (darla DESIGN.md:546-585).
    - Runs survive session death and are recovered with `gauntlet-collect.sh`, never re-run
      (25:MULTI_AGENT.md:499-523).

## 3. What did not work / wasted time (evidence)

### Method failures
- **Incremental tuning against your own lineage plateaus.**
  - bc22: about 62 accepts, yet the latest bot only went 50/50 against snapshots 15 or more
    accepts older (22:progress/vs_old_bots_history.csv).
  - bc26: the every-5th roster showed the current bot losing to g_iter16, seven accepts back
    (26:TRAINING_LOG.md:9498-9515).
  - bc25: §5b found the accepts were sub-additive (25:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:594-616).
- **Synthetic archetypes forked from our own code went stale three times.** That inflated win
  rates, once showing 62.5% as 95.0% (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:437-445,
  26:tools/resync_archetypes.py). Once re-synced, they were mirror matches stuck at 50%.
- **Accepting or rejecting on a lopsided instrument.** Iteration 63 was accepted on benchmarks
  and then retracted at 33% on the mirror. Iteration 78 was "closed" on benchmarks and later
  accepted at +4 (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:308-322).
- **Treating deterministic results as binomial.**
  - One candidate was rejected as "inside the noise floor" when identical code splits exactly
    20/40 with zero swept maps (25:MULTI_AGENT.md:408-480).
  - The error bar is over *maps* (`25:tools/map-resample.py`).
  - Swept maps are near noise-free. Carol found 44-56% of maps split by spawn side alone
    (25:agents/carol/LEARNINGS.md:38-57).
- **Arms that never executed.**
  - 162/162 identical games, several times (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:324-339).
  - A `sed` that matched nothing produced a byte-identical copy and 72 forfeits that read as a
    "catastrophe" (25:agents/darla/tools/make-arm.sh:2-17).
  - A registered falsifier that read a counter the arm didn't contain (DESIGN.md:3823-3836).
- **Dormant-branch reasoning.** Three bc26 iterations in one day were built on correct logic
  inside branches that never fire: gates above the treasury's equilibrium band, conditions nested
  inside `nearestCat != null` (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:567-610).
- **Comparing across different map sets.** A "breakthrough" from 0/60 to 6/162 was really a switch
  from 10 maps to 27 (26:TRAINING_LOG.md:4006-4030). Comparisons against a wrong or stale
  baseline cost bc22 a whole thread (22:TRAINING_LOG.md:5060-5077), and bc26 had a stale-control
  error too (26:tools/paired-check.sh:4-17).
- **Optimising a mechanism metric that doesn't convert.** In bc26, five mechanism-verified damage
  increases converted to nothing. Halving the death rate by leashing units cost 18 peer games
  (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:873-889). darla optimised its own "wasted move" proxy directly and
  lost (DESIGN.md:3915).
- **"Helps the diagnosed case, hurts broadly."** This recurred across three subsystems in bc22,
  because conditions that look narrow on one replay are common everywhere (22:LEARNINGS.md:318-330).
- **Overfitting the fixed evaluation pool.** On darla's paired census, the held-out estimate came
  out at about 60% of the in-pool estimate, after about 23 arms judged on the same 75 maps
  (darla DESIGN.md:5380-5450).
- **Long single-area grinds.**
  - bc22 spent about 10 of 23 iterations on income-side fixes for a turn-order starvation
    problem (22:LEARNINGS.md:402-414).
  - Sage micro was a no-op because the bot never had Sages (:331-346).
  - camelcase stayed at 0/20 through dozens of tactical iterations (:520-527).

### The bc25 multi-agent setup: mostly overhead
- **Joint local optimum.** Three isolated lineages spent weeks contesting a 3-point gap between
  each other while losing about 75% to the external yardstick (25:OBJECTIVE.md:26-50). The
  tournament standings are zero-sum: bob fell from 95.7% to 34.7% purely because the others rose
  (25:tournaments/HISTORY.md, 25:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:708-722).
- **Token cost.**
  - Agents were 83% of the day's token spend, with contexts running at 700k-965k
    (25:MULTI_AGENT.md:26-52).
  - A cold start read about 5,200 lines (about 77k tokens) before doing anything useful; 13 cold
    starts in one day cost about 1M tokens (:294-306).
  - The project kept hitting account usage limits, and bob was retired "to fit within usage
    limits" (:56-58).
- **Isolation engineering.** About 300 lines of rules covered:
  - shared scratchpad `tasks/` and root leaking sibling transcripts and replays
  - the shared VM home holding 738 entries, 416 of them runner scripts
  - process listings leaking arm names (:142-222)
  - an isolation-sweep daemon and a `u=wx` chmod on the scratchpad root (:172-191)
- **Duplicate sessions of one lineage happened five times**, including to the coordinator that
  wrote the rule. "Completed" is a snapshot, and messaging an agent resumes it. Two sessions forked
  the ledger into two iteration 45s and exceeded the job cap (:224-292).
- **Shared git index races.** 1,716 lines were committed under the wrong lineage. The fix was
  `tools/agent-commit.sh` with a private index (:576-617).
- **Doctrine bloat.** TRAINING_ALGORITHM grew to 841 lines, METHODS.md to 86 entries,
  METHODS_EVIDENCE to 2544 lines, plus per-agent LEARNINGS archives of 2.4-3.4k lines. Carol's
  learnings had to be split because reading them at every cold start cost about 33k tokens
  (25:agents/carol/LEARNINGS.md:1-9). The methodology got sharper and the bots did not get
  stronger until the rewrite.
- **What did transfer usefully.** Cross-lineage *measurements*, *closures* and *realised discount
  factors* (25:MULTI_AGENT.md:84-140). The best idea was carol's unadopted siege fork, which only
  became usable once the isolation was dropped.

### Things that looked textbook and failed
- Pure bug-nav replacing greedy movement: −14 (darla DESIGN.md:3823).
- Symmetry inference that needed travel to eliminate candidates was a no-op guess. The
  terrain-in-vision version was void on bytecode (DESIGN.md:2956-3050).
- bc22 tried tiebreaks toward the centre, random, and per-robot-random; each narrowed the
  side-bias gap but regressed overall, because a consistent arbitrary preference gives formation
  cohesion (22:LEARNINGS.md:263-278).
- Commanded multi-unit coordination failed four times in bc26: focus fire, sweeps, squeak
  coordination (26:RESEARCH.md:112-115).

## 4. Tooling worth porting

Caveat for 2019: the prior engines were Java (javap, raw `java -cp battlecode.server.Main`,
FlatBuffers replays). First establish what 2019's engine, language and replay format are, then
port the *function* of each tool, not the code.

**Year-agnostic (port the design, rewrite the code):**
- **Gauntlet runner**: (opponent, map, side) games run in parallel, bypassing the build-system
  daemon. It writes `results.csv` (opponent, map, side, winner, rounds, result, win_type), a
  summary, and loss replays.
  - 26:tools/gauntlet.sh (header :2-19). Plain ssh instead of `gcloud compute ssh`, which stalls
    under load (:26-31).
  - 25:tools/gauntlet.sh (raw `java -cp` at :175-189). Warns loudly on a duplicate in-flight run.
- **compare_gauntlets.py**: a game-by-game diff over the shared keys only, with round deltas
  (26:tools/compare_gauntlets.py, 22:tools/compare_gauntlets.py).
- **Paired head-to-head and McNemar on discordant keys**:
  - 25:agents/darla/tools/head-to-head.sh: candidate against an explicit reference, all maps,
    both sides
  - paired-roster.sh: candidate against N frozen bots on all maps
- **map-resample.py**: bootstrap and jackknife over maps. Always report from the launched bot's
  own perspective (25:tools/map-resample.py:1-25).
- **paired-check.sh**: a 4-match pre-screen on two maps of opposite character, with the control
  always re-measured, never quoted (26:tools/paired-check.sh:1-30).
- **make-arm.sh**: creates a variant and *proves* it differs. The package line must be renamed,
  the diff must be non-empty, and the intended change must be present
  (25:agents/darla/tools/make-arm.sh).
- **arm-runner.sh + idle-filler.sh**: a setsid queue under flock that skips finished arms, plus an
  idle fallback job that runs fresh random-map roster samples (25:agents/darla/tools/).
- **accept-iteration.sh**: freeze, promote, promotion test, benchmark, push, as one script
  (25:agents/darla/tools/accept-iteration.sh).
- **Snapshot tooling**: `snapshot.sh` freezes `src/bot` to `src/g_iterN` (26:tools/snapshot.sh).
  `bot_identity.py` labels each run by build content, which matters because history rows were
  mislabelled 4 times (25:tools/bot_identity.py, 26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:743-747).
- **Progress charts**: `track_vs_old_bots.py --roster` derives every-Nth snapshot automatically
  (26:TRAINING_LOG.md:2535-2550), plus `plot_vs_old_bots.py`, `plot_progress.py` (cumulative
  accepts) and `plot_alt_metrics.py`.
- **gauntlet-collect.sh**: recovers a run whose driver session died (25:tools/gauntlet-collect.sh).
- **Replay-to-text dumper** with these modes: per-round team metrics CSV, `--indicators`,
  `--all-actions`, `--moves`, `--robot <id>`, terrain dumps, win type, and shared-pool counters.
  - 22:tools/bc22_replay.py was Python with a synthetic-replay test suite
    (`test_bc22_replay.py`).
  - 26:tools/replaydump/ReplayDump.java is Java against the engine's own generated classes; see
    26:tools/README.md for the struct-union and "alive kings field is really kings+10·cheese"
    quirks.
  - Rule: **capture a mechanism census while the replays are fresh**, because pruning deleted
    them (darla DESIGN.md:800-805).
- **engine-facts.md**: each fact recorded with the command that re-verifies it
  (25:tools/engine-facts.md). Digests can agree and still be wrong, so only the source counts
  (25:MULTI_AGENT.md:125-131).
- **Bytecode monitor in iteration 0.** Compare the round before and after a turn and
  `Clock.getBytecodeNum()` against the limit, shown in the indicator string. Watch for silent
  indicator truncation (22:LEARNINGS.md:59-81).

**Infrastructure / ops (port if we run on GCE again):**
- 22:CLOUD_DRIVER.md: an always-on `claude-driver` e2-small running tmux plus a `battlecode-dev`
  compute VM started on demand. 25:RESTART_SESSION.md and 25:WIPE-RUNBOOK.md.
- 25:tools/systemd/: timers instead of cron, because of DST (25:MULTI_AGENT.md:350-357).
  `driver-prune.sh` and `vm-prune.sh` handle disk; one queue nearly died of a full disk
  (darla DESIGN.md:827).
- 25:.claude/settings.json and README: allow routine commands, `ask` for network, installs and
  irreversible actions. Move dangerous routine steps inside checked scripts rather than widening
  permissions.

**Not worth porting:** the multi-agent machinery (`agent-commit.sh`, `isolation-sweep.sh`,
`agent-watchdog.sh`, the semaphore, `tournament.sh`), unless we deliberately run more than one
lineage. METHODS.md as-is is too heavy, so use the short rules in §6 below instead.

## 5. Cross-year principles (from the RESEARCH syntheses; year-agnostic)

Sources: 22:RESEARCH.md, 26:RESEARCH.md, 25:reference/RESEARCH.md. These were already filtered of
2019 material. Note that bc25's copy also strips 2025 post-mortems (25:reference/README.md:11-29).
**Warning: summarising post-mortem PDFs through web-fetch produced fabricated quotes.** Extract
the text and read it (26:RESEARCH.md:13-16, 25:reference/RESEARCH.md:412-415).

**Economy / scarce resources**
- A "resource" means: having none of it is near a loss, you can remove it from the opponent, and
  the opponent can deny it to you. **Unit count is its own currency**
  (22:RESEARCH.md:168-199, 25:reference/RESEARCH.md:310-337).
- Build an explicit exchange rate between resources, even a linear one (22:RESEARCH.md:203-218).
- Denying production (blocking a spawner, "tainting") is often cheaper than direct combat
  (22:RESEARCH.md:181-189).
- Overproducing a cheap unit causes congestion near spawn, so throttle on local density
  (22:RESEARCH.md:194-199).
- From our own runs: in all three years the strong opponent won on **production scale and opening
  tempo**, not per-unit quality (22:LEARNINGS.md:378-414, darla DESIGN.md:5825-5864, 9794-9850).
  - Spending is the investment. Withholding production banked nothing (darla finding #6).
  - Never gate a paint-costly action on a currency gate (finding #7).
  - Never set a constant above its resource's operating band (DESIGN.md:43-49).

**Navigation**
- Greedy step, with bug-nav as a *fallback*, plus a past-turn stack to escape concave obstacles
  (22:RESEARCH.md:50-87, 25:reference/RESEARCH.md:195-230). Further options:
  - an unrolled fixed-radius BFS (r² ≤ 20) for local movement
  - simulating left vs right turns on hard cases
  - treating friendlies as soft obstacles (walls about 75% of the time)
  - a random tiebreak as a loop-breaker
- With binary passability, use bugnav; with variable cost, use greedy. A* or full BFS usually
  blows the bytecode budget (26:RESEARCH.md:128-134).
- Use a persistent heading rather than "nearest target", because units get blocked by robots more
  than by walls (darla DESIGN.md:47-49).

**Exploration**
- Use repulsion between explorers, applied only if the other explorer is at least as close to
  unexplored ground, plus a momentum term (22:RESEARCH.md:291-300).
- Scout toward the map centre by default (22:RESEARCH.md:112-114).
- A spiral scout tuned for a central spawn lost a final on a distant-resource map
  (22:RESEARCH.md:353-356).

**Symmetry**
- Maps are always rotational, horizontal or vertical. **Infer which, don't assume.** Gone Fishin'
  assumed rotational and it backfired, and bc22 assumed it too: wrong on 40% of its maps
  (22:LEARNINGS.md:82-91, 22:RESEARCH.md:91-114).
- Eliminate candidates using terrain *already in vision*, not by travelling to mirror points,
  which made darla's version a no-op guess. Check only the informative half to save bytecode
  (25:reference/RESEARCH.md:234-249, darla DESIGN.md:2956-2984).
- Separate from this: **play-symmetry of our own code**. No fixed-compass tiebreaks, no
  "first result" from fixed-order sensing scans, no Team.A/B, and no shared fixed-seed RNG
  correlating across teams (22:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:5-47, 22:LEARNINGS.md:92-113).

**Combat micro**
- One team's estimate: "Macro +5%, micro +30-50%" (22:RESEARCH.md:118-164).
  - Kite after every attack, unconditionally.
  - If you can attack, attack, even blind at the last-known position.
  - Prefer axis-aligned moves on ties.
  - Retreat only at critical HP, and as a group.
  - Target by fewest hits to kill.
  - Take free moves onto better terrain after attacking.
- **Look for radius asymmetries** where one unit's action range exceeds another's vision. They
  recur every year (22:LEARNINGS.md:24-33, 25:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:46-48).
- Caution from our runs: check unit-count and production parity before blaming micro
  (22:LEARNINGS.md:388-401).

**Communication**
- Design the schema first: decide the *units of information*, then the bit layout
  (22:RESEARCH.md:15-46).
  - Sector-coarse coordinates (7 bits vs 12) and `loc mod 128`.
  - Log-encode wide-range values.
  - Batch writes through a local buffer with dirty flags.
  - Keep a lazily refreshed local cache.
- Our addendum: **name the badly-made decision before designing any schema**
  (25:reference/RESEARCH.md:187-191).
- Shared-array flags gated on "X hasn't happened recently" get stuck open in a real war. Verify
  that they clear (22:LEARNINGS.md:145-161).
- Census means live counts, not cumulative (:162-170).

**Compute / bytecode budget**
- Limits are historically about 7.5k-15k per robot, so even a 20×20 BFS can eat a whole turn
  (26:RESEARCH.md:128-131).
- Use custom bytecode-aware structures only *after* a working version exists to profile
  (22:RESEARCH.md:317-322).
- Overruns are silent and mid-instruction, so monitor them forever (22:LEARNINGS.md:59-73).
- A corner-clustered HQ once ran out of bytecode scanning before it recorded its siblings, which
  lost a qualifier (25:reference/RESEARCH.md:132-137).
- Instrumented builds can approach the limit even when the shipping build doesn't
  (25:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:473-478).

**Meta-game / scrimmage behaviour**
- Infrastructure first: nav, resource gathering, comms, testing scripts and test maps. These
  survive strategy changes (26:RESEARCH.md:81-87, 22:RESEARCH.md:304-337).
- Simplicity and robustness beat sophisticated coordinated plans, per the 2020 winner
  (26:RESEARCH.md:89-99).
- Copy and adapt others' tested code openly, then improve on it (26:RESEARCH.md:183-189).
- Every strong team builds a parallel local match runner (26:RESEARCH.md:24-33).
- Live scrimmages outrank frozen old bots, and you need both (26:RESEARCH.md:35-75).
- Don't fix everything a replay shows you. Prioritise by expected win-rate gain
  (26:RESEARCH.md:101-105).
- Adapt per game. Measure distance, passability and resource density and branch rush vs turtle;
  don't fix an identity at compile time (22:RESEARCH.md:222-246).
- Balance patches kill thin-edge strategies, so keep mechanics decoupled from strategy
  (22:RESEARCH.md:250-274).
- **No last-minute submissions without a test run.** One team threw twice this way
  (26:RESEARCH.md:160-162).
- "Code > Elo": don't hard-code against ladder opponents, and beware the gap between scrim maps
  and tournament maps (26:RESEARCH.md:164-170).
- Emergent coordination beats commanded coordination. Spawn-order formation won about 2/3 of
  self-play games with no messages at all (25:reference/RESEARCH.md:286-306).

### Recurring self-inflicted process errors (each happened more than once)
1. **Lessons written but not enforced.** "A lesson you wrote is not a control." The same error
   recurred within the hour, and the coordinator duplicated agents five times after writing the
   rule (25:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:325-333). Put checks into scripts, as with `make-arm.sh`,
   `accept-iteration.sh` and gauntlet duplicate detection.
2. **Wrong baseline or referent.**
   - an opponent's number compared to another opponent's baseline (22:TRAINING_LOG.md:5070-5077)
   - a stale control quoted from the log (26:tools/paired-check.sh:12-17)
   - map-set mismatch (26:TRAINING_LOG.md:4006)
   - a resampler reporting the opponent's score (25:tools/map-resample.py:14-25)
   - Always re-measure the control.
3. **Accepting on the instrument you pre-registered while an unrun cheap one disagrees.** This
   nearly shipped a −10-game regression (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:657-691).
4. **`git add -A` and uncommitted snapshots.** These nearly baselined unverified code twice, and a
   g_iter19 sat uncommitted (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:732-741). Stage explicit paths and commit
   in the same step as the accept decision.
5. **Assuming instead of reading the opponent source or the engine.** bc22 misattributed which
   benchmark used the gold/Sage economy (22:LEARNINGS.md:475-483). One spec PDF contradicted
   itself, and the engine source settled it (26:reference/README.md).
6. **Reasoning about code without checking reachability, choice-set size, or the outer guard**
   (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:567-610, 25:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:420-444).
7. **Selecting replays only from losses**, which is tautological for scoring terms. In a mirror,
   differences between teams are positional, not policy (26:TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md:420-433).
8. **Bundled changes and joint accepts left attribution unresolved.** darla's first accept was
   two constants at once, and attribution was still owed afterwards (DESIGN.md:1640-1660).
9. **Methodology as displacement activity.** bc26 had a whole day with "no bot improvement; the
   output is methodological" (26:TRAINING_LOG.md:6602-6620), and bc25 produced 86 METHODS
   entries. Measurement hygiene is necessary, but the strength gains came from a rewrite,
   opening tempo and production scale.

### Recommendations for 2019 (synthesis of the above)
- **Phase 0, before strategy:**
  - spec digest in `RULES.md`
  - engine-source probes
  - a determinism check
  - a parallel headless runner with a replay dumper
  - bytecode or compute monitor in iteration 0
  - a play-symmetry audit
  - map-symmetry inference planned in
- **Get a reference opponent near our level early.** All three projects lacked an
  ability-calibrated diverse opponent, and in bc25 the one at parity (`v3`) was the most useful.
  Build simple archetype poles (rush, economy) that are *independent* of our own code, so they
  can't go stale or collapse into mirrors.
- **Accept gate = paired head-to-head against the last accepted build on the full map pool**,
  counting discordant (map, side) keys and swept maps. Also:
  - run a frozen every-5th roster on fresh random maps on a schedule
  - track win type and the round of the first key milestone (opening tempo)
- **First diagnostics on any loss to a stronger bot:**
  - where are games decided (tally win types)?
  - the opening timeline in the first 100 rounds
  - unit count and production rate against the opponent
  - only then per-unit micro
- **Stall triggers** (any of them fires: change area, do an API sweep, ablate, or rewrite):
  - 3 rejects in one area
  - a flat roster
  - "nothing incremental left"
  A rewrite from measured findings is cheap and has the best track record.
- **Keep docs lean.** Use a short LEARNINGS plus a closure ledger with re-open conditions. Logs
  are append-only and grep-only. Never stop to wait, and keep the machine busy with a queue plus
  a filler.
